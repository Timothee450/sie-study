3.1

Bond basics

$1,000 parCouponMaturityCurrent yield

Four things define every bond

Par valueWhat you get back: assume $1,000 par
Coupon rateYearly interest as a percent of par, fixed for life
Maturity dateThe day the issuer repays par
IndentureThe contract with all the terms; a trustee enforces it

Interest comes from par, not price

Try it
Par
$1,000
Interest a year
$50
Each semiannual payment
$25
Interest = 5% × $1,000 par = $50 a year
  • Assume $1,000 par unless told otherwise
  • Corporate bonds pay twice a year
  • A quote of 95 means 95% of par: $950 (one point = $10)
  • Move the price: the interest doesn't budge

Price up, yield down

Try it · 5% bond, $50 a year
Current yieldAnnual interest ÷ market price
  • Discount (below par): yield > coupon
  • Par: yield = coupon
  • Premium (above par): yield < coupon

Exam tipPrice and yield always move in opposite directions.

Rates up, bond prices down

5% bond · years to maturity
Price buyers will pay
$1,000.00

0.00%
  • Your coupon is fixed; new bonds pay the market rate
  • Rates up → your bond looks weak → price falls to a discount
  • Rates down → your bond looks strong → price rises to a premium
  • Switch to 30 years: the price swings much further

Exam tipLonger maturity and lower coupon mean more price sensitivity to rates.

Callable bonds and yield to call

1

Rates fall

The issuer can borrow more cheaply than its 6% coupon.

2

Bond trades at a premium

A 6% coupon is attractive, so the price rises above par.

3

The issuer calls

It repays the call price early and refinances at a lower rate.

4

Yield to call

The return if the bond is called at the first call date.

Exam tipFor a premium bond the lowest yield is usually the yield to call. The lowest possible yield is the yield to worst.

Issuers and ratings

CorporateTreasuryMunicipalRatings

Three big borrowers

CorporateBacked by the company's ability to pay

Interest is taxed by federal, state and local governments.

U.S. TreasuryFull faith and credit of the U.S. government

Federal tax only. Exempt from state and local income taxes.

MunicipalA state or local government

Interest is generally exempt from federal income tax.

Exam tipAgency bonds (Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, Ginnie Mae) are a fourth group. Each issuer gets its own chapter later.
